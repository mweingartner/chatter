# Chatter 3.4.1 clip endings — 2026-11-12

3.4.0 shipped with a click at the end of Scar's clips, reported by its first listener within the hour. Its verification had judged endings by the level of a clip's last 20 ms, which a burst of noise followed by silence passes. Endings are now judged at every fall into digital silence, at 5 ms resolution: whether the sound fades or is cut, and whether a sound shorter than 60 ms stands alone in silence.

- 200 jobs through the `chatter-engine` helper (50 replies per voice, rendered and streamed, 1.7B model): no failure, no second attempt, no simulated playback hole; transcripts as in 3.4.0. Scar: no cut and no burst in 100 clips (3.4.0: 9 cuts, 11 bursts), and the loudest final 5 ms of any clip 40 dB below her speech (3.4.0: 21 dB). JARVIS: 1 of 100 clip ends still counted as a cut, 38 dB below his speech (3.4.0: 13, up to 22 dB below); the cuts counted inside his clips are his clean recording going quiet in pauses, at least 41 dB below his speech.
- 810 tests passed with `CHATTER_TEST_MLX=1`, three of them new for the ending. One existing timing test (`longPassagesTrimInLinearTime`) exceeded its ceiling once while the suites ran in parallel and passed alone and on a second run; it exercises code this change does not touch.
- Still not a listening test by anyone but the user.

# Chatter 3.4.0 recorded-voice verification — 2026-11-12

Validated on a Mac Studio (M3 Ultra, 512 GB), macOS 27.3, Xcode 27.2, with the user's own Scar and JARVIS recordings. Nothing here is a listening test: audio was judged by on-device speech recognition and signal measures, and by comparison with the official PyTorch implementation. Whether a voice still sounds like its speaker is for its owner to hear; a before-and-after set was left for that.

- **808-test suite passed** with `CHATTER_TEST_MLX=1`: 451 core, 166 audio, 91 engine (the Metal-backed Qwen tests included), 77 tooling and 23 system-speech tests. New tests cover the decoder's attention window and restorable streaming state, the sampler, the encoder's position layout, speech-activity monitoring, passage attempts and seeds, streamed edge trimming, the soft ending, playback pacing and the recorded-voice model setting.
- **Parity with the reference** ([QWEN_PORT.md](QWEN_PORT.md)): encoder codes identical to PyTorch for both recordings (all sixteen codebooks, 286 and 417 frames); decoder audio within 1e-5 relative error, whole and streamed; speaker embedding cosine 1.000000; talker logits within BF16 precision over 361 teacher-forced frames.
- **Settings chosen by measurement:** about 3,000 generated passages across 17 named variants of sampler, prompt layout, decoder and model, 96 per variant and voice.
- **The engine as shipped:** 735 jobs through the `chatter-engine` helper over its protocol: 50 assistant-style replies per voice as renders, streamed play and saved files, on both models (300 before and 435 after the last adjustment to the speech monitor, which at first generated one- and two-word replies again for no reason). No job failed; no passage ran on, ended within 25 dB of its speech level, or paused longer than 0.6 s. In the final run of 400, the recognizer transcribed 99 of the 1.7B model's 100 distinct replies as written, apart from how it writes numbers and compounds ("6:42" as "642", "river bank" as "riverbank"); in one of Scar's it heard "cost" for "costs". Of the 0.6B model's 100 it transcribed 96 as written: Scar added an "uh", said "clouds" for "cloud" and the same "cost", and JARVIS read "6:42" as "six to forty-two". One 0.6B passage that did not start was generated again and then did; no 1.7B passage needed a second attempt. The same request always produced the same file, across runs and across a rebuild. Simulated playback of the streamed chunks' arrival times never ran dry mid-passage; the first chunk arrived after a median 0.36 to 0.50 s by voice (1.7B) and 0.25 s (0.6B).
- **The packaged app,** launched with isolated data on another port under its engine sandbox: 21 jobs over HTTP (both recorded voices at every quality, Ryan and Aiden, a saved file, a two-voice dialogue) and three spoken on the Mac, all completed.
- **The installed app:** the same eight replies were rendered by 3.3.3 before the update and by 3.4.0 after it. 3.3.3's set held a 13.5 s rendering of a 5 s reply (a 7.5 s silence inside it) and a Studio-quality passage that ended in sound; 3.4.0's has neither, and its longest pause is 0.4 s.

Not covered: Macs with less memory or a slower GPU (the automatic model choice is a memory threshold, not a measured speed), languages other than English, voices other than Scar, JARVIS and a few passages from Ryan and Aiden, designed voices, and paces other than 1.0.

# Chatter 3.1.0 security verification — 2026-10-01

Validated locally on Apple Silicon with macOS 27 and Xcode 27 beta. These are development-build checks; no notarized 3.1.0 release is claimed.

- **504-test suite passed:** 340 core, 38 engine, 50 audio and 76 tooling tests. One optional transcription test requiring a private recording was skipped. Fixtures remain synthetic.
- **Constrained execution:** the full suite also passed with a single cooperative worker. The first hosted runs exposed an older-compiler type-checking limit and blocking verification calls that could starve a small executor. Assertions were preserved, process/stdio waits were moved off the cooperative pool, and CI now separates compilation from a five-minute test stage.
- **Service boundaries:** 26 packaged-app checks cover authenticated HTTPS, certificate pinning, rejection of the administrator token on LAN, missing/expired/revoked credentials, browser origins, scope restrictions, client-isolated receipts/retries/cancellation, per-client queue capacity, authentication before body buffering, and service availability under 128 incomplete connections.
- **Actual engine isolation:** subprocess tests prove that model reads and staged writes work while credential/personal-file reads, writes outside permitted directories, and network connections fail. These checks caught and corrected a kernel-path mismatch in the first policy implementation. This is a process policy, not full App Sandbox qualification.
- **Real audio:** Ryan narration and Ryan/Aiden dialogue completed under the restricted helper. Generated WAVs were owner-only, native 24 kHz mono PCM24; streamed downloads matched local files byte for byte, and dialogue turn timing was retained. These are format/runtime checks, not subjective voice-quality acceptance.
- **Storage and compatibility:** migration preserves receipt IDs and per-owner retries; regressions cover pending payload limits, exclusive process locking, retained exports, duration limits, atomic private WAV creation, and changed-model integrity verification. The live preferences review exercised migration of the existing local library without including its contents in the repository.
- **Supply chain and distribution:** all vendored file hashes and 29 OSV queries passed locally with no reported affected pins. The audit produces a component inventory, not a guarantee of exhaustive advisory coverage. Production packaging rejects missing signing/notarization credentials before building. GitHub private vulnerability reporting is enabled.

Local evidence is retained under the ignored `artifacts/security-hardening/` directory and associated build/test logs. Public binaries from 3.0.2 predate this hardening. Developer ID distribution credentials remain unavailable on this machine; no new public binary is claimed.

## Chatter 3.0.2 public release verification — 2026-09-30

Validated on an Apple Silicon M5 Max, macOS 27, Xcode 27 beta. Deployment target is macOS 26; a separate macOS 26/minimum-memory machine was not available for qualification.

- **488-test suite passed:** 328 core, 38 engine, 49 audio, and 73 tooling tests. The optional private-recording transcription test was skipped because no private fixture was supplied. Audio format tests use generated tones only.
- **Dependency independence:** all 12 Swift packages resolved to local filesystem paths with outbound networking denied, from a new scratch directory. Native MLX C/C++ source is populated, and there are no Git submodules or LFS pointers. A clean production build completed using the vendored graph. Upstream native compiler warnings and SwiftPM's unused direct-override warnings remain; the build is not claimed warning-free.
- **Archive and runtime:** extracted the app ZIP to a different directory, verified its ad-hoc signature, and launched it with isolated data, a separate loopback port, login registration disabled, and a system-only PATH. With no models present, it reported the expected setup-required state and created exactly Aiden and Ryan with no recordings.
- **Real synthesis:** restarted that extracted app against existing pinned model files and passed the eight-tool MCP/schema/capability checks for Aiden and Ryan. Each generated a completed Studio WAV with optimistic tone and a separate delivery instruction. The verifier checked native 24 kHz mono PCM24 output. This verifies runtime packaging, not a new 15.6 GB download or subjective voice quality.
- **Local-only listener:** a fresh launch exposed Network.framework EINVAL when a fixed port was supplied in both requiredLocalEndpoint and NWListener's initializer. A four-case native probe isolated the duplicate-port error. Chatter now supplies that port once; the extracted app's local-only HTTP/MCP checks passed.
- **Privacy:** public source starts from a clean snapshot. Prior personal development history, models, recordings, generated narration, API tokens, and diagnostic reports are excluded. An older format-test speech clip was replaced by reproducible mathematical tones in all nine formats. Vendored cryptography test keys and tiny tensor fixtures are public upstream test data, not credentials or speech weights.
- **Binary contents:** all four executables are arm64 and depend dynamically only on system/Swift libraries. Required Metal and SwiftPM resource bundles, MIT/third-party notices, and portable plugin files are included. App/plugin ZIP audits reject runtime data, recordings, model weights, and Python caches.

The first public release is **not notarized**: this machine has no Developer ID distribution identity. Signature integrity, live runtime success, GitHub publication, and Apple notarization are separate claims. No lower-memory performance guarantee, Intel support, cloud-client tunnel, or end-user listening approval is asserted.
